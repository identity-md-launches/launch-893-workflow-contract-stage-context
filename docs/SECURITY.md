# Security and independent review handoff

This is the implementation contributor's assessment, not an independent review. No deployment or live oracle proof was exercised. An independent contributor must review the accepted contracts and final manifest before release.

## Trust and design limits

- The configured Gold address must be the accepted `LaunchToken`. Constructor code-existence checks do not establish token identity. Typed ERC-20 transfers check boolean returns, and funding checks the exact balance delta. The game intentionally supports this ordinary Gold token, not arbitrary rebasing, fee-on-transfer, no-return or callback-enabled assets.
- The coordinator must be the real, verified Chainlink VRF v2.5 implementation for the target chain. Only its address may submit a result. The constructor accepts a nonzero external address without executing or checking its code, so isolated constructor checks can run without a live chain. External verification of its code and identity is essential: an EOA coordinator could submit arbitrary callbacks, while an absent/incompatible coordinator cannot service requests. The game does not itself check a VRF proof or permit a user-supplied random seed. A malicious coordinator selected at deployment could choose outcomes; tests using a mock are not evidence of production randomness.
- The subscription owner controls funding and consumer registration externally. There is no application owner or ability to replace its coordinator. Funding failures roll back request creation and can be retried only while no request has been accepted. Once a request is accepted, the game cannot request another for that round.
- **Timeout tradeoff requiring release review:** [Chainlink's security guidance](https://docs.chain.link/vrf/v2-5/security) discourages cancellation because discarding unfavorable results can bias outcomes. This game explicitly prioritizes principal recovery after a fixed deadline: a party capable of withholding fulfillment or censoring it until that deadline can cause a refund instead of settlement. There is no caller-selectable cancellation window or reroll; callbacks and expiry have non-overlapping timestamp bounds, and a timely result can never expire. Those restrictions remove an ordinary last-moment transaction-ordering race but do not remove oracle/sequencer censorship or subscription-management risk. This is a material availability/fairness assumption, not a claim of unconditional fairness.
- Sequencers/validators may influence transaction inclusion, ordering, timestamps and reorgs. Use a sufficiently long timeout and confirmation depth for the chain and maximum exposed pot. No value-at-risk cap is imposed by this implementation. Entries are visible and other miners can respond before the deadline.
- Exactly one ticket per tile per wallet does not prevent Sybil entries. Covering all tiles guarantees a share of a winning tile but not profit relative to stake. Rewards have no guaranteed market value, and sponsors receive no return rights.
- Anyone can spend keeper gas to open rounds or progress them. Empty rounds do not spend oracle subscription funds. Nonempty rounds do; a participant can repeatedly pay entry gas/stakes to consume subscription funds, potentially recovering its stake in single-player rounds. The subscription operator must budget and monitor this permissionless usage. The round duration bounds the frequency of accepted requests, not the total cost.
- Donations are permanent, no claim expiry exists, and nobody can recover abandoned claims or mistakenly transferred assets. There is no emergency pause or upgrade path. A replacement deployment cannot migrate funds without user claims. Choosing these immutability properties accepts operational limitations.

## Accounting and adversarial behavior

Each successful entry raises both its round's remaining native amount and global native liability by `msg.value`. The pot is never derived from contract balance. Reserving a round moves Gold from available to reserved without transferring it. Settling never transfers funds. Empty/timed-out rounds move their entire Gold reserve back to available; each player retains a native refund equal to purchased tickets times the immutable price.

Claims validate the caller's recorded selections, mark the claim, deduct all liabilities and reserve balances, then perform transfers. OpenZeppelin's storage-based reentrancy guard protects every public mutation that may participate in a payout/funding/request call chain. The authenticated callback performs no external calls and only stores a result for an already bound pending request. Unknown, late and duplicate callbacks do not mutate a round. A synchronous fake callback while requesting has no bound request and cannot settle anything.

Both payout transfers are atomic: a native recipient rejecting payment rolls back the earlier Gold transfer as well. A failing recipient does not block settlement, other claimants or the next round. All terminal claims sum to the original entry pot and reserved Gold; the last winning claim receives division dust. Work per entry is bounded by 25 tiles, and settlement/claims do not loop over participants.

Direct donations and forced native currency are deliberately excluded from liabilities and payout formulas. No owner, keeper or arbitrary caller can sweep either liabilities or surplus. The factory deployment test verifies that the entire Gold supply remains at the factory after constructing the application, and scans both real runtimes for the protected forbidden opcodes and size limit.

## Validation record and limits

The delivered tests include example success/failure cases, 256-case fuzz properties for transfers, constructor rejection and grid outcomes, and a stateful accounting invariant with 128 runs at depth 64. The handler interleaves funding, opening, entry, time advancement, close, callback, settlement, expiry and old-round claims. Its model counts actual transfers and sums every round's liabilities, rather than merely asserting that one balance decreases.

The production callback is tested within the constructor's minimum 100,000-gas allowance. The mocked request/fulfillment path also tests failure, out-of-gas delivery, delayed results, malformed results, unauthorized callbacks, and reused IDs. This does not benchmark a live coordinator's total gas or fee behavior.

Foundry's build-time lint emits heuristic warnings for timestamp comparisons, checked casts, external calls and recipient-selected native transfers. Here timestamps implement deadlines rather than entropy; `% 25` fits in `uint8`; truncating a hash to a four-byte tag is intentional; funding checks actual received tokens; guarded claims update state before external transfers; and only the entitled caller can choose its payout address. These patterns were inspected and exercised by the failure tests. Warnings are not suppressed as proof of safety. Slither, Mythril, a fork test, formal verification, and live proof verification were not run.

Before release, resolve and independently review the actual coordinator, subscription control, chain support, constructor economics, confirmation/timeouts, and the completed manifest. Publication, attestation, admission and deployment are service responsibilities; their later completion is not claimed by this source deliverable.
